# Common-Cyber-Threats-

## Define:
### threat
A threat is any potential danger that could exploit a vulnerability in a system,leading to harm such as data theft,service disruption,or unauthorized access.

example: A malicious activity withthe aim of stealing data or damaging systems. 

How to protect yourself: Stay informed about new threats amd follow cybersecurity news.

### Vulnerability
A vulnerability is simply a weak spot in a computer system, software, or process that attackers can use to cause harm. 

example: You leave your Wi‑Fi router on its default password “Admin123”. That weak spot is the vulnerability attackers could exploit.

How to protect yourself: Change default settings and patch software regularly.

 ### Attack
 A cyberattack is an attempt to exploit a vulnerability in order to steal data, disrupt services, or gain unauthorized access.

 Example: Hackers usually break into your weak password. 

 How to protect yourself: Respond quickly to suspicious activity.
 

## Explain:
### Phishing 
Phishing is a type of cyberattack where criminals pretend to be trusted sources (like banks, email providers, or delivery companies) to trick you into giving away sensitive information such as passwords, credit card numbers, or clicking harmful links.

Example: You get an email that looks like it’s from your bank, asking you to “verify your account” by clicking a link. The link goes to a fake site that steals your login.

How to protect yourself: Hover over links before clicking.

### Malware 
Malware (short for malicious software) is any program or code created to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to computers, networks, or data. In simple terms, it’s like a digital germ that infects your device and causes trouble.

Exampe: You download a “free game” from a shady website. Hidden inside is malware that spies on your keystrokes and steals your passwords.

How to protect yourself: Install antivirus and keep systems updated.

### Ransomeware 
Ransomware is a type of malware that locks or encrypts your files and demands payment (a ransom) to unlock them.

Example: You open an email attachment that looks like an invoice. Suddenly, all your files are locked, and a message demands Bitcoin to unlock them.

How to protect yourself: Patch vulnerabilities and  Keep offline/cloud backups.

### Social Engineering 
Social engineering in cybersecurity is when attackers manipulate people into giving away sensitive information or access, instead of directly hacking computers.

Example: Someone who can get your passwords and information just by talking to you psychologically. 

How to protect yourself: Always verify identities and never share credentials over phone/email.

### Password attacks 
password attacks specifically target the human habit of using weak, reused, or guessable passwords.

Example: Using weak or Guesable passwords making it easier for attackers to acces you personal infomation. 

How to protect yourself: Use strong unique passwords.


### Dos/DDos
A DDoS attack (short for Distributed Denial of Service) is when attackers flood a website, server, or network with so much traffic that it becomes overwhelmed and stops working properly.

Example: A hacker uses thousands of infected computers to flood your favorite online game server with traffic. The server crashes, and no one can play.

How to protect yourself: Use firewalls.
